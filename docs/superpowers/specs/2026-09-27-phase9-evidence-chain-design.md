# Phase 9 V1 — Evidence Chain and Decision Layer Design

**Status:** design contract for human review; no Phase 9 implementation exists at this checkpoint.
**Design date:** 2026-09-27.
**Base:** Data Layer V1 implementation and deterministic acceptance complete; live acceptance remains blocked by the recorded Deribit host environment issue. Base commits `be2187e7e6f83c745ea112278525576e9db8adc4` and `dae27ec01a94fe6298de6a6d7419c94ad8241965`.
**Intent:** turn a small set of persisted Stage 1 candidates into reproducible, explainable `DecisionCandidate` records suitable for a later Phase 10 risk review. No Phase 9 decision is an order or a prediction of profit.

## 1. Scope and authority

Phase 9 consumes only Phase 1–8 canonical rows and context already persisted by the Data Layer. It does not scan the full universe or call exchange REST/WebSocket APIs, chain RPCs, news sources, Deribit, or any other public data provider. Jev is a conflict reviewer over a bounded public-market summary; it cannot fetch evidence or make the final decision. Versioned Python validation, pattern matching, and decision policy retain final authority.

The only output handed to Phase 10 is a `DecisionCandidate` and its audit references. Phase 9 has no entry price, leverage, sizing, stop loss, take profit, order type, private API, execution, position, account, or balance interface. `TRADING_MODE=paper` remains a prerequisite. No trade is a valid result.

V1 covers short intraday opportunities in the 15m–4H range and exactly three pattern families, each with LONG and SHORT structures: `TREND_CONTINUATION`, `BREAKOUT_CONFIRMATION`, and `LIQUIDATION_REVERSAL`. There is no generic pattern DSL, marketplace, full-universe scorer, multi-model council, automatic policy tuning, or dealer GEX. This document freezes interfaces and decision semantics. A committed immutable `PHASE9_POLICY_MANIFEST_V1` must contain typed predicate cutoffs with units, decimal normalization and rounding rules; per-source freshness; each pattern/timeframe/direction truth table; required, supporting and conflicting evidence; hard-veto precedence; soft-degradation behavior; confidence ceilings; Jev-required conflict classes; TTL; revalidation cadence; material-change predicates; and runtime bounds. The manifest is identified by version **and** canonical content hash. No numeric weighted score, bullish/bearish count, hidden confidence score, or runtime tuning is permitted. Until every predicate, cutoff, unit, rounding rule and conflict precedence for a pattern is frozen and reviewed, that pattern cannot be `MATCHED`; its status is `NOT_CONFIGURED` or `NOT_MATCHED` and it cannot create an eligible candidate. Missing or unknown manifest versions fail closed.

## 2. Existing system fit and ownership

The current Engine in `src/quant_phase1/entrypoints/engine.py` runs Stage 1 every 300 seconds by default. `persist_stage1()` creates a `screening_runs` row and upserts `screening_results`; the Engine connection context commits those writes. `screening_results` is unique on `(run_id, symbol)`, references `screening_runs` with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and current writes do not emit an outbox event. The existing `outbox_events` table has `id`, `event_type`, `aggregate_key`, `created_at`, `published_at`, and JSONB `payload`; repository search found no current publisher or consumer. There is no Stage 1 cleanup call in the current writer, but the foreign-key relationship means a future run cleanup could remove it. These are implementation gaps, not reasons to depend on source-row lifetime.

The chosen topology is the existing Engine plus PostgreSQL. A `Stage1CandidateEventV1` outbox artifact is inserted on the same PostgreSQL connection and transaction as the Stage 1 run/result write. Transaction commit therefore makes the candidate result and its durable event visible together; rollback makes neither visible. Outbox persistence is an internal durable handoff, not an external broker. A bounded Engine poller reads unacknowledged Phase 9 event rows after restart. Any post-commit notification is only a wake-up optimization; correctness comes from polling and database state. The event contains an immutable Stage 1 candidate projection sufficient for admission and audit even if the source row is later upserted or deleted. The Phase 9 EvaluationSnapshot separately freezes the Phase 1–8 evidence projection. No new container, broker, provider client, or Phase 1–8 interpretation change is part of the design.

Only category A or B, `screening_results.status=AVAILABLE`, a valid `screening_runs` row, and an in-scope `symbols` contract may be admitted for evaluation. Every A/B result change emits its durable event with its actual status; non-AVAILABLE or out-of-scope events receive a durable rejected disposition. Category C/D remain Stage 1 outcomes and do not create Phase 9 Candidate events. B is allowed into intake but gains no automatic eligibility. In V1 `stage1_candidate_id` and `screening_result_id` are the stable `screening_results.id` (BIGINT); the event and snapshot also record `(run_id, symbol)`, the complete selected Stage 1 projection and its content hash. Phase 9 normalizes the persisted `symbols.category=USDT-FUTURES`, USDT quote and perpetual contract into `market=USDT_PERPETUAL`; it does not guess market from a symbol suffix. Evaluation timeframe is a Phase 9 `15m|1H|4H` policy dimension, never retroactively inserted into Stage 1.

## 3. Intake, identity and bounded scheduling

`Stage1CandidateEventV1` has `event_id`, `event_type`, `event_schema_version`, `stage1_candidate_id`, `screening_result_id`, `symbol`, normalized `market`, `candidate_created_at`, `candidate_valid_until`, `stage1_policy_version`, `source_as_of`, bounded `source_refs`/snapshot refs, `canonical_payload_digest`, and `created_at`. In V1 the two candidate/result IDs are equal to `screening_results.id`. Its immutable candidate projection includes the persisted A/B category, classification, status, reason codes, bounded `inputs_used`/indicator/structure projection and exact source references needed to explain Stage 1. It does not embed full market payloads. The canonical event payload is at most 64 KiB; exceeding this bound is a persistence/contract failure and does not silently truncate evidence. `stage1_policy_version` is `screening_runs.rule_version`; `source_as_of` is the screening run's UTC timestamp. Candidate validity uses an explicit versioned intake TTL; an unconfigured TTL disables admission rather than inventing an expiry.

Canonical event serialization is versioned UTF-8 JSON: object keys sorted lexicographically; arrays sorted only where their contract is set-like, while ordered evidence/source sequences keep their defined order; UTC timestamps in fixed ISO-8601 `Z` form with microseconds; decimal values as normalized base-10 strings without exponent notation, with trailing fractional zeroes removed and all zero forms encoded as `0`; explicit null distinct from absent; and enums as exact stable strings. Binary floats, duplicate JSON keys, non-finite numbers and unsupported values reject the event. `canonical_payload_digest` is SHA-256 over canonical event content excluding `event_id`, the digest field and delivery metadata. `event_id` is a deterministic SHA-256-derived identifier from event type, schema version, result ID and payload digest. The idempotency key is `(event_type, event_id)`; a future additive unique index on `(event_type, aggregate_key)` uses a stable aggregate key containing result ID plus digest. Neither identifier depends on a publish or consume attempt.

The Phase 9 poller pages `published_at IS NULL` event rows by increasing outbox ID with a fixed per-cycle limit. For this event type, `published_at` means the durable Phase 9 intake handoff is complete; it does not mean a broker publish. The poller first commits an admission ledger row for every allowed timeframe, including explicit deferred/rejected dispositions, then marks the event handed off. If it crashes after the Stage 1 transaction commits, restart finds the event. If it crashes after admission commit but before setting `published_at`, replay hits the same event/timeframe idempotency keys and creates no duplicate evaluation or decision. Retention preserves unacknowledged outbox events. Once acknowledged, Phase 9 stores the event identity and immutable candidate projection in its own ledger; later outbox cleanup cannot alter replay. A duplicate delivery is harmless through the same keys and single-flight claim. The scan never queries the full `symbols` universe. A separate bounded revalidation scheduler considers only admitted candidates and ACTIVE Phase 9 decisions. Bootstrap is permitted only from the latest still-fresh A/B run and must use the same durable event ledger; it is not a full-universe scan.

An evaluation identity is deterministic over `stage1_candidate_id`, `timeframe`, UTC evaluation window, policy generation, and material-change generation. Unique constraints plus a per-key single-flight claim prevent duplicate work across concurrent notifications or restarts. Duplicate notifications attach to the existing evaluation. Material input changes arriving while a key is RUNNING are coalesced into one following generation, not silently discarded. A completed evaluation is never reopened to replace a recorded Jev outcome; a newly available judgment requires a new evaluation generation. Different symbols may run concurrently within configured limits. Priority is fresh Stage 1 intake, then material-change revalidation, then scheduled revalidation. Admission deferral is durable; expired input produces an explicit terminal record. Queue entries contain IDs, not full market payloads.

The ledger separates `intake_disposition=ADMITTED|DEFERRED|EXPIRED|REJECTED|DEDUPLICATED|SUPERSEDED` from evaluation state. Accepted or deferred work has evaluation state QUEUED; expired/rejected/superseded work is CANCELLED with an explicit reason. Evaluation states are `QUEUED`, `RUNNING`, `WAITING_JEV`, `COMPLETED`, `DEGRADED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED`; they are operational states, separate from decision lifecycle. A lease and attempt count allow startup recovery of interrupted RUNNING/WAITING_JEV work. The same evaluation identity and snapshot hash must be reused on retry. A changed input creates a new material generation. Bounded retries are at most two after the first attempt for retryable DB serialization or gateway transients; hard contract failures do not retry automatically.

## 4. Fixed EvaluationSnapshot and time rules

Each evaluation first takes one short PostgreSQL `REPEATABLE READ` transaction, fixes `as_of` in UTC, reads a bounded projection for one `(stage1_candidate_id, timeframe)`, and durably persists an immutable `EvaluationSnapshot` before computation proceeds. It closes that transaction before any AI call or significant CPU work. All later Evidence/Pattern/Decision computation and replay uses the stored projection only; it never re-selects a mutable source row to reconstruct an earlier decision. The projection records the actual normalized semantic inputs for every Phase 1–8 source used or explicitly unavailable, with `source_phase`, `source_type`, `source_ref`, symbol, market, event/observed time, capture time when relevant, availability/freshness/quality/coverage state, source schema/version, projection version and canonical projection digest. The snapshot has `evaluation_id`, immutable Stage 1 candidate artifact, timeframe, `evaluation_time`, `as_of`, ordered projections, policy hashes and full running Git SHA. The source reference proves provenance; the canonical projection provides replay data.

Selection requires a source event/observed time appropriate to that source **and** availability time (`processed_at`, or a documented canonical receive/fetch field when `processed_at` is absent) no later than `as_of`. Rows available after `as_of` are future information even if their exchange timestamp is older. A missing event/observed timestamp is never filled from `as_of`. Missing, invalid or explicitly unverified times are carried with their timestamp semantics; they cannot satisfy a core freshness requirement. A core projection without reliable time fails closed as evaluation failure or a policy hard veto; optional context becomes explicitly degraded/unavailable. Existing mutable/upserted rows are copied as bounded normalized values into the durable snapshot; later update or retention deletion cannot change an evaluated decision. If a source was already unavailable before snapshot formation, core evidence fails closed and optional evidence degrades. The system never substitutes newer data for an old missing row. Raw bodies and credential-bearing URLs are excluded. Row references alone are not sufficient audit evidence.

Freshness is source-specific and versioned. Price tickers use the Phase 1 age rule (current 5 seconds); 5m/15m/1H/4H candles use expected-closed-bar logic with their configured ingestion grace, never a flat 180-second age. OI and funding use their Phase 2 freshness/status contract (current 180-second source limits), with actual interval and normalization. Flow and liquidation windows use the expected closed window, source gap, coverage and quality status. Phase 5 contexts retain context timestamp, input-window end and coverage. Phase 6 uses the configured news/macro/unlock horizons, and a disabled source is NOT_CONFIGURED. Phase 7 uses event time, finality, coverage and source status. Phase 8 uses each metric's own source timestamp, capture time, unit status and coverage; a context generated every 15 minutes does not refresh an older OI/volume value. Policy failure or unavailable timestamps fail closed for required evidence. No universal freshness age is defined.

Canonical hash serialization uses sorted object keys, stable contract-defined array order, UTC timestamps in fixed ISO-8601 `Z` form with microseconds, exact normalized decimal strings without exponent notation, explicit null and status values, UTF-8 and a versioned canonicalization identifier. It rejects binary floats and non-finite numbers in canonical semantic projections. `evaluation_snapshot_hash` includes the immutable Stage 1 candidate identity/projection, ordered canonical evidence projections, source status/coverage, projection/schema versions, policy manifest versions and full code SHA. It does not depend on database physical row order, JSON insertion order, or a source row's later value. Final `input_snapshot_hash` is SHA-256 over the fixed evaluation snapshot hash plus the validated durable JevReview ID/content digest, or an explicit `NO_REVIEW`/`REVIEW_NOT_AVAILABLE` sentinel and cause. Jev is evaluated after the snapshot, so it cannot be part of the initial snapshot hash. Hash serialization is versioned; retry and replay must reproduce it byte-for-byte.

Before final persistence, Python rechecks Stage 1 validity, core freshness and deadline against the current clock. If a long Jev wait makes required inputs stale, the evaluation becomes DEGRADED/FAILED with a reason and a fresh evaluation is scheduled; it must not emit an apparently current eligible decision from expired evidence.

### LIQUIDATION_SOURCE_CONTRACT_V1

The canonical projection contains distinct `transport_status` (`AVAILABLE|DEGRADED|RATE_LIMITED|NOT_AVAILABLE|ERROR|UNKNOWN`), `availability_status` (`AVAILABLE|STALE|NOT_AVAILABLE|PARTIAL|ERROR`), `coverage_status` (`SOURCE_DECLARED_COMPLETE|PARTIAL|UNKNOWN|NOT_AVAILABLE`), and `gap_status` (`KNOWN_GAP|NO_KNOWN_GAP|UNKNOWN`), plus `gap_count` when known, `window_start`, `window_end`, `observed_at`, `source/provider`, `aggregation_semantics`, `source_ref` and `projection_digest`. Successful provider transport and row `availability_status=AVAILABLE` never imply complete coverage. `PARTIAL_AGGREGATED` always maps to PARTIAL; unsupported or absent coverage semantics map UNKNOWN; a missing health/gap observation cannot become `NO_KNOWN_GAP`. An absent row means no observation, not zero. A zero is valid only when the source explicitly reports zero over a fresh source-declared-complete interval and the expected interval/gap checks pass. Completeness is scoped to the named provider/window, never the entire market.

For reversal, partial coverage can contribute context but its Evidence strength is at most MODERATE. A policy may allow `PARTIAL_MATCH`, or a `MATCHED` pattern with confidence at most MEDIUM only when all other required predicates pass. A stale required interval, unknown required side, unresolved gap crossing the required interval, or inadequate coverage selected as a hard-veto predicate prevents MATCHED. The exact window/freshness cutoffs live in the immutable policy manifest; no fallback numeric rule is permitted.

## 5. EvidenceItem and EvidenceChain contracts

`EvidenceItem` is immutable and includes `evidence_id`, `evaluation_id`, `symbol`, `evidence_type`, `semantic_code`, `direction` (`BULLISH|BEARISH|NEUTRAL|MIXED|UNKNOWN`), `strength` (`STRONG|MODERATE|WEAK|UNKNOWN`), `observed_at`, `availability_status` (`AVAILABLE|STALE|NOT_AVAILABLE|PARTIAL|ERROR|NOT_CONFIGURED`), `freshness_status` (`FRESH|STALE|UNKNOWN|NOT_APPLICABLE`), `quality_status` (`VALID|PARTIAL|INVALID|UNKNOWN`), optional `coverage_status` (`SOURCE_DECLARED_COMPLETE|PARTIAL|UNKNOWN|NOT_AVAILABLE`), `source_phase` (`PHASE1|PHASE2|PHASE3|PHASE4|PHASE5|PHASE6|PHASE7|PHASE8`), `source_ref`, `provider`, bounded `provenance`, `raw_refs`, `interpretation`, `schema_version`, and `created_at`. The deterministic `evidence_id` hashes the evaluation snapshot, type, semantic code, scope and ordered source refs. `raw_refs` contain identifiers/hashes, not raw provider payloads. Evidence direction is a semantic label, never a +1/-1 vote. Missing and UNKNOWN are separate from NEUTRAL; unavailable IV or OI is not zero.

V1 `evidence_type` is exactly `PRICE_STRUCTURE`, `OPEN_INTEREST_STRUCTURE`, `TRADE_FLOW`, `LIQUIDATION_CONTEXT`, `FUNDING_BASIS_POSITIONING`, `MARKET_REGIME`, `OPTIONS_CONTEXT`, `ONCHAIN_SPOT_MACRO`. Semantic codes are registered with an evidence-schema version; examples include `TREND_UP/DOWN`, `BREAKOUT_UP/DOWN`, `PRICE_UP_OI_UP/DOWN`, `BUY_FLOW_DOMINANT`, `FLOW_DIVERGING_PRICE`, `DIRECTION_NOT_AVAILABLE`, `LONG/SHORT_LIQUIDATION_CLUSTER`, `LONG/SHORT_CROWDED`, `BTC_RISK_ON/OFF`, `PUT/CALL_SKEW`, `SPOT_BUYING_CONFIRMATION`, and `NEWS_EVENT_CONTEXT`. An unknown code is a schema failure, not a neutral input. A new code requires a versioned schema/policy change.

The immutable `EvidenceChain` contains candidate symbol/ID, evaluation ID/time/timeframe, ordered `supporting[]`, `conflicting[]`, `neutral[]`, typed `missing[]`, `degraded[]`, `hard_vetoes[]`, `matched_patterns[]`, `jev_review_required`, evidence-schema version, `evaluation_snapshot_hash` and final `input_snapshot_hash`. The builder creates an in-memory draft from the fixed EvaluationSnapshot; after optional Jev validation, the final hash is calculated and the chain is inserted once in the same final transaction as the decision. The draft is not a persisted audit record. Every listed evidence ID resolves to an item in the same evaluation. Missing entries retain required type, source status, reason and source/capture times. A degraded item retains coverage/gap/unit details. The chain preserves conflicts; it cannot be reduced to an indicator count.

## 6. Python validator and quality policy

Validation occurs before pattern evaluation and again at persistence. It checks UTC, symbol/market mapping, row identity and digest, source phase/provider provenance, as-of ordering, status, per-source freshness, required units, gap/finality/coverage, bounds, and schema/policy versions. `DATA_CORRUPTION_OR_PROVENANCE_INVALID` is a hard failure if identity or source mapping is untrustworthy; it does not become a soft unavailable item.

V1 `HARD_VETO` codes are `CORE_MARKET_DATA_INVALID`, `STAGE1_INVALIDATED`, `CORE_DIRECTIONAL_EVIDENCE_MISSING` (only for the pattern whose required directional input is absent), `DATA_CORRUPTION_OR_PROVENANCE_INVALID`, `EXTREME_REGIME_CONFLICT` (only the exact predicate in the versioned manifest), and `EVIDENCE_TOO_STALE`. The manifest defines each predicate and precedence; ambiguous or unknown precedence fails closed. A veto makes `eligible=false` and remains visible in the chain/decision. Ordinary bearish BTC context is not an automatic veto. An input-contract or DB failure may instead terminate the evaluation without a DecisionCandidate; the evaluation records FAILED and the Phase 9 health reflects the fault. A policy veto is a valid audited no-trade decision, not a system error.

`SOFT_DEGRADE` covers absent/stale auxiliary Options, on-chain, Macro/News/Unlock NOT_CONFIGURED, non-required secondary PARTIAL, temporary RPC trouble reflected by Phase 7, and Jev unavailability when review is optional. It records a code and caps/reduces confidence according to the manifest truth table; it does not itself set `eligible=false`. If a particular pattern explicitly requires the missing category, that pattern cannot be MATCHED; only an explicitly versioned veto predicate can elevate it to a hard veto. No source status is recoded to make an output pass.

## 7. Evidence patterns: required facts and conflicts

The V1 policy manifest lists for each `(pattern_type, timeframe, LONG|SHORT)` its `required`, `supporting`, `contradicting`, `hard_conflict`, freshness/coverage gates and applicable cutoff versions. Trend uses 1H and 4H; breakout and liquidation reversal use 15m and 1H. The six directional structures are mirrored, but never inferred by simply negating a failed opposite structure. Each predicate evaluates to `TRUE|FALSE|UNKNOWN`; the fixed precedence is: invalid schema/provenance terminates evaluation as FAILED; missing reviewed manifest produces `NOT_CONFIGURED`; an applicable hard veto blocks eligibility; a known required contradiction produces `CONFLICTED`; unknown/missing required facts produce `PARTIAL_MATCH` when the setup predicate is true and otherwise `NOT_MATCHED`; all required predicates TRUE with valid freshness/quality and no disqualifying conflict produces `MATCHED`. Supporting evidence changes only the manifest-defined conflict/degradation/confidence state; its count never votes. A global veto may coexist with an otherwise matched pattern, but the DecisionCandidate remains ineligible.

| Pattern | Required structure for LONG (SHORT mirrors) | Supporting context | Conflicted or failed examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| TREND_CONTINUATION | Existing uptrend; comparable OI stable/rising; confirmed buy flow; valid price bars and no hard regime veto | Relative strength, sector, non-extreme funding | CVD sells against rising price, severe long crowding, or missing confirmed flow prevents MATCHED |
| BREAKOUT_CONFIRMATION | Closed-bar upside structural break; healthy/rising comparable OI; confirmed buy flow and non-divergent CVD | Short liquidation event, relative strength | Breakout with falling OI, falling CVD and extreme long funding is `FAILED_BREAKOUT_RISK`; it does not create SHORT |
| LIQUIDATION_REVERSAL | Sharp fall, real long-liquidation event with known side and explicit coverage, fading sell flow, OI contraction/stabilization, stopped lower lows, no versioned systemic-crash veto | Spot recovery and regime support | Continued new lows, renewed sell flow, unknown liquidation side, stale required window, or hard-veto gap blocks MATCHED |

Only Phase 3 legally directional sources can satisfy flow predicates. A `DIRECTION_NOT_AVAILABLE` or UNKNOWN side can be retained as context but cannot satisfy buy/sell flow. Phase 4 liquidation semantics follow `LIQUIDATION_SOURCE_CONTRACT_V1`: `PARTIAL_AGGREGATED` and any known gap remain coverage PARTIAL regardless of transport or row availability. Partial liquidation evidence can never have `strength=STRONG`; the manifest may permit `PARTIAL_MATCH` or `MATCHED` only with all other required predicates satisfied and `confidence_band <= MEDIUM`. A gap, stale window, or inadequate coverage that meets the manifest hard-veto predicate prevents MATCHED. It never asserts complete-market liquidation. Funding classifications come from normalized Phase 2 observations, not guessed raw scales. Any numeric threshold is a named, immutable policy-manifest parameter with source/unit semantics; a missing parameter prevents MATCHED.

`PatternMatch` has `pattern_type`, `direction`, `status` (`NOT_CONFIGURED|MATCHED|PARTIAL_MATCH|CONFLICTED|NOT_MATCHED`), ordered required/supporting/conflicting/missing evidence IDs or typed missing records, vetoes, and `pattern_policy_version`. `NOT_CONFIGURED` means no reviewed manifest exists for that exact pair; it is never eligible. `MATCHED` requires every required predicate, valid provenance/freshness and no disqualifying conflict. `PARTIAL_MATCH` has the price setup and some support but lacks a required fact without a known contradiction. `CONFLICTED` has a setup plus a known contradictory required fact or competing directional structure. `NOT_MATCHED` lacks the setup. Only MATCHED can become eligible. Jev may explain conflict but cannot convert a missing required fact or veto into MATCHED.

## 8. Jev conflict review through Phase 6 AI Gateway

Jev is called only for evidence conflict, pattern ambiguity, directional pattern conflict, or materially contradictory auxiliary context. Clear evidence makes no AI call. The Phase 9 adapter constructs a strict, bounded public-market allowlist and `PromptEnvelope`/`AIRequest` for the existing `quant_phase6.ai.AIService`, reusing its bounded queue, timeout, retry, rate limiter, budget, cache and telemetry. The existing `StrictSchema` checks top-level required/allowed fields and enums; it does not provide the nested Phase 9 Evidence ID/array/length checks required below. Repository inspection found no explicit circuit breaker. Phase 9 adds no second provider stack and no generic fallback council; `fallback_provider=None` in V1. The Phase 6 `build_safe_context()` currently accepts an `ExternalEvent`; Phase 9 must add a domain-specific safe-context builder that produces the existing `AISafeContext` contract. These missing adapters/tests are an implementation gap. Until Phase 9 task ownership, safe context, nested validation, durable review linkage and timeout/retry/rate/budget behavior are wired and verified, `PHASE9_REAL_JEV_INTEGRATION_STATUS=NOT_CONFIGURED`; no existing circuit breaker is assumed.

`JevReviewRequest` contains review ID, symbol, timeframe, pattern type, bounded supporting/conflicting/degraded/missing summaries, market regime, evidence-schema and prompt versions, requested-at UTC and snapshot hash. It excludes raw provider payloads, API credentials, private account IDs, balances, positions, orders and private trade history. Validation recursively checks the request allowlist and secret-bearing values before gateway submission; the serialized request is at most 64 KiB. The output is a strict nested object with review ID; `relation=CONSISTENT|MOSTLY_CONSISTENT|CONFLICTING|HIGHLY_CONFLICTING|INDETERMINATE`; `conflict_severity=NONE|LOW|MEDIUM|HIGH`; `dominant_context=FLOW_CONFIRMATION|FLOW_DIVERGENCE|CROWDING_RISK|REGIME_CONFLICT|LIQUIDATION_CONTEXT|PRICE_STRUCTURE_DOMINANT|MIXED|UNKNOWN`; bounded supporting/conflicting assessments, unresolved conflicts, degradation notes and short auditable `reasoning_summary`; provider/model/model-version, prompt/evidence-schema versions and created-at UTC. V1 hard limits are output <=32 KiB, nesting depth <=4, at most 64 Evidence IDs in each reference list, at most 16 items per assessment/conflict/degradation array, each assessment text <=256 characters, and `reasoning_summary` <=512 characters. Validation covers enum values, nested object and array element schemas, reference existence in this evaluation's EvidenceChain, unknown-field policy, all limits, and prohibited trade instructions. A Jev reference to an Evidence ID not present in the current chain is invalid. Unknown or trade-instruction fields (`BUY`, `SELL`, entry, stop, take profit, leverage, `position_size`, order) reject the judgment and cannot reach policy. No chain-of-thought is requested or stored.

Jev can relate existing evidence IDs only. It cannot invent a new evidence item, change a Phase 3 UNKNOWN side into buy/sell, change source status, or approve a veto. NOT_CONFIGURED, timeout, rate limit, budget denial, invalid JSON/schema or provider outage yields `jev_status=NOT_AVAILABLE` with the distinct underlying cause (including `provider_lifecycle=NOT_CONFIGURED`) preserved in the audit row if a review was requested. If a manifest class requires Jev for a HIGH conflict and real Jev is NOT_CONFIGURED/unavailable, Python fails closed: `eligible=false`, `confidence_band=INSUFFICIENT`, reason code `UNRESOLVED_HIGH_CONFLICT`; no fallback approval is allowed. Lower conflict follows the versioned Python conflict matrix and may remain ineligible or be eligible with a lower band, but never defaults to approval. Real Jev is not required for deterministic implementation/replay or paper readiness when no mandatory unresolved conflict remains; Fake/Recorded Jev uses the same nested validation path without network access.

Before any `DecisionCandidate` can reference a review, the validated `JevReview` row and its request/result digests, status, model/prompt/schema versions, bounded safe summary and evaluation ID must be durably committed. The decision stores only that durable `jev_review_id`. If a response arrives but JevReview persistence fails, the evaluation is FAILED and no decision is written. If the review commits but a later decision transaction fails, the durable unattached review remains an auditable orphan for that evaluation; retry may reuse it only when request hash and exact validated output digest match.

## 9. Python DecisionCandidate policy

`DecisionCandidate` is immutable and includes `decision_id`, `evaluation_id`, `stage1_candidate_id`, `symbol`, `market`, `timeframe`, `created_at`, `valid_until`, `eligible`, `direction_bias=BULLISH|BEARISH|NEUTRAL`, `confidence_band=HIGH|MEDIUM|LOW|INSUFFICIENT`, nullable `matched_pattern`, `pattern_status=NOT_CONFIGURED|MATCHED|PARTIAL_MATCH|CONFLICTED|NOT_MATCHED`, ordered supporting/conflicting/degraded evidence IDs, typed missing evidence, veto reasons, nullable durable `jev_review_id`, structured `reason_codes[]`, bounded `short_summary`, `input_snapshot_hash`, `evidence_schema_version`, `pattern_policy_version`, `freshness_policy_version`, `decision_policy_version`, `ttl_policy_version`, `prompt_version` (explicit `NONE` if no review), full Git `code_version`, and nullable `supersedes_decision_id`. `decision_id` is deterministic over evaluation identity and final input hash. No `confidence_score`, probability, win rate, PnL, Sharpe or order fields exist.

Confidence describes evidence completeness, agreement and quality only. HIGH requires a MATCHED structure, all core sources available with valid provenance and no material degradation. MEDIUM allows a MATCHED structure with explicitly permitted soft degradation; partial liquidation caps at MEDIUM. LOW means a directional reading exists but conflict/coverage is inadequate. INSUFFICIENT means no reliable direction, a veto, or unresolved mandatory conflict. `eligible=true` requires MATCHED, HIGH/MEDIUM, no hard veto, still-current Stage 1/core data and no unresolved HIGH conflict. Otherwise it is false. `eligible=false, direction_bias=BULLISH` is valid: the direction is intelligible, but the case cannot pass to Phase 10. With no defensible direction, emit `NEUTRAL/INSUFFICIENT`.

Python selects an eligible direction only when one directional structure satisfies required predicates and the opposite does not produce a material unresolved conflict. It never counts bullish versus bearish items or sums +/- weights. If both directions are credible or a breakout fails, it records conflict and no trade rather than automatically reversing direction. `reason_codes` are registered, versioned machine codes (for example `PRICE_BREAKOUT`, `OI_CONFIRMATION`, `FLOW_CONFIRMATION`, `FUNDING_CROWDING_MODERATE`, `OPTIONS_UNAVAILABLE`); `short_summary` describes those codes without replacing them.

## 10. TTL, material change and lifecycle

The initial policy manifest proposes TTLs of 20 minutes for breakout, 15 minutes for liquidation reversal, 45 minutes for 1H trend and 90 minutes for 4H trend. These are operational starting values, not statistical truths. Revalidation cadence is 5 minutes for breakout/reversal, 10 minutes for 1H trend and 15 minutes for 4H trend. All are versioned/config-driven and bounded by the scheduler budget. TTL is chosen by Python from pattern and timeframe; Jev cannot set or extend it.

Core DecisionCandidate fields are never updated. Lifecycle is an append-only stream of `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `INVALIDATED` events. Effective status also checks `now >= valid_until` even if an expiry event is delayed; Phase 10 must reject an expired candidate without waiting for the event writer. Early invalidation follows Stage 1 invalidation, core stale/invalid data, versioned material regime reversal, core evidence supersession or a new decision. A new Decision B records `supersedes_decision_id=A`; inserting B and A's SUPERSEDED event is one transaction. A remains unchanged.

Material changes are direction, eligibility, confidence band, matched pattern, hard-veto set, semantic core evidence, or a material Jev judgment. Tiny numeric drift without a semantic change does not create another decision. A revalidation with the same final semantic projection updates its evaluation outcome/dedup record but emits no new DecisionCandidate. Status events and new decisions have unique event identities so restart cannot double-expire or double-supersede.

Implementation requires a new additive migration after existing 001–015; this design task creates no migration. Important query/filter fields are columns, with bounded detail in JSONB. The planned tables are:

|---|---|

| `phase9_evaluation_snapshots` | Immutable evaluation FK, as-of UTC, bounded normalized selected-source projection, ordered refs/status/times, hash and policy/code versions. One fixed snapshot per evaluation identity. |

All Phase 9-to-Phase 9 foreign keys are restrictive. Phase 9 audit tables do **not** use cascading foreign keys to Data Layer rows that may be upserted or retained away. Stable source IDs, content digests and the bounded canonical projection are copied into the snapshot. Index `(stage1_candidate_id, timeframe, created_at DESC)`, `(symbol, created_at DESC)`, ACTIVE status event lookup, evaluation lease/state, and unique idempotency keys; add a partial unique/indexed Phase 9 identity and unpublished-ID lookup on existing `outbox_events`. No unbounded JSON search is required for the runtime path. V1 hard per-record limits are at most 64 EvidenceItems, 16 source rows per category, 256 KiB normalized EvaluationSnapshot, 64 KiB EvidenceChain detail and 32 KiB Jev output. The durable Stage 1 outbox has at most 1,024 unacknowledged rows and at most 64 MiB unacknowledged payload bytes. At either cap, the Stage 1 result+outbox transaction rolls back and the collector records `PHASE9_OUTBOX_BACKPRESSURE` through a separate bounded health/error path **after** rollback, so the signal itself is not lost with the rejected transaction; it never drops or truncates a Candidate event. After recovery, the next Stage 1 run can create a fresh candidate event. Acknowledged rows may be cleaned only after the Phase 9 admission artifact is durable. Raw market bodies are never stored in Phase 9.

The implementation migration must install an UPDATE/DELETE rejection trigger on immutable Phase 9 audit tables and grant the runtime role INSERT/SELECT but no mutation privilege there. Only the operational `phase9_evaluations` row changes state/lease; its final outcome and reason remain queryable. Runtime migrations remain owned by the established migration role. A successful evaluation first durably persists its fixed snapshot, performs bounded computation and optional Jev outside a DB transaction, and durably persists any validated JevReview before decision construction can use it. It then inserts evidence items, final chain, pattern matches, DecisionCandidate and initial/supersede status events in **one** short DB transaction. Failure rolls back that final transaction. A crash before it leaves a recoverable evaluation with no half-decision; a previously committed JevReview may remain unattached and auditable. A hard validation failure terminates the evaluation with a durable failure reason, not a fabricated candidate.

## 12. Runtime, backpressure, failure and health

The Engine owns all Phase 9 tasks and cancels/drains them during shutdown. The Phase 9 supervisor has bounded queue and one owner per task; no unbounded `asyncio.create_task`. `PHASE9_RUNTIME_POLICY_V1` hard ceilings are 2 in-flight evaluations, 32 queued evaluation IDs, 8 MiB queued bytes, 30 seconds per evaluation, 1 concurrent Jev call, 16 revalidations per minute and 2 retry attempts after the first attempt. Runtime configuration may lower these bounds; increasing them requires a reviewed policy-version change and measured resource evidence. The existing process-local `WorkAdmissionController` and cross-process `PostgresWriteAdmission` govern Phase 9 Engine evaluation work and each short persistence transaction. Register `phase9.evaluations` as `RECOVERABLE_REPLAYABLE` in the existing stream contract and attach it to one bounded Phase 9 Engine source entry; no additional scheduler/service or write bypass is introduced.

Backpressure defers or coalesces recoverable work in the durable evaluation ledger. Every admitted candidate resolves to evaluated, rejected, vetoed, expired, failed, deduplicated or admission-deferred with a reason. No silent drop and no in-memory-only obligation. Source/network/provider errors remain as the Data Layer reported them; Phase 9 does not attempt direct provider recovery. HARD failures are invalid core data, inconsistent snapshot, invalid provenance, DB persistence failure, unknown policy/schema version or canonical identity violation. They stop the current evaluation; persistence failure makes Phase 9 aggregate health ERROR. SOFT failures are optional context missing/PARTIAL and optional Jev unavailable; they can yield an explicitly degraded decision. RETRYABLE failures are bounded serialization conflicts or transient AI Gateway conditions. Neither timeout nor budget exhaustion means success.

Use the existing `LifecycleState` and `DataState` vocabulary. Phase 9 intake, evidence builder, validator, pattern engine, Jev reviewer, Python decision engine, persistence and revalidation scheduler each expose status/reason/timestamp. A single Phase 9 source entry with bounded component detail fits the current observability source-count cap; registration in existing `SourcePhase`/`SourceId` is future additive work. With Python and persistence healthy and no required Jev review, Jev NOT_CONFIGURED can leave aggregate AVAILABLE; while a requested review is unavailable it becomes PARTIAL/DEGRADED unless the unresolved conflict makes that evaluation ineligible. Persistence ERROR makes aggregate ERROR. Never invent GOOD/BAD/MAYBE statuses.

Per-evaluation diagnostics include ID, symbol, Stage 1 ID, `queue_wait_ms`, `snapshot_build_ms`, `evidence_build_ms`, `validator_ms`, `pattern_ms`, `jev_ms`, `decision_ms`, `persist_ms`, `total_ms`, evidence/missing/degraded/veto counts, Jev called/status, decision result and normalized failure category. Metrics are `phase9_evaluations_total`, `phase9_decisions_eligible_total`, `phase9_decisions_rejected_total`, `phase9_hard_veto_total`, `phase9_soft_degrade_total`, `phase9_jev_calls_total`, `phase9_jev_failures_total`, `phase9_queue_depth`, `phase9_evaluation_latency`, `phase9_revalidation_total`. Labels are bounded; no IDs, symbols or secrets become high-cardinality metric labels. Existing Engine cap remains 384 MiB; Collector 256 MiB and PostgreSQL 768 MiB are not increased. A capacity failure is classified as working set, query amplification, queue/Jev backlog, file cache or leak before changing any budget.

## 13. Deterministic replay and acceptance

`PHASE9_REPLAY_V1` is layered on the frozen `DATA_LAYER_REPLAY_V1`; it does not change Data Layer acceptance semantics. Its manifest references the Data Layer fixture/replay bundle identity and content hash, Stage 1 Candidate fixture, immutable EvaluationSnapshot canonical projections, `as_of`, policy manifest versions/content hashes, full code SHA, and a Recorded Jev judgment when required (or the explicit no-review/unavailable outcome). It runs the Phase 9 Evidence Builder, Validator, Pattern Engine and Python Decision Policy over those persisted inputs. It never calls a real model or provider. Deterministic comparison covers Evidence, EvidenceChain, PatternMatch, DecisionCandidate and `input_snapshot_hash`, repeated across restarts. A separate **MODEL EVALUATION** mode may compare a current model's assessment of the same evidence; its output is not deterministic acceptance evidence and cannot overwrite the recorded decision.

Required tests: Stage 1 result plus outbox transaction atomicity; crash after Stage 1 commit and poller restart; duplicate delivery dedup; event survives source-row update/delete before admission; each source adapter and status mapping; source delete after EvaluationSnapshot then identical replay; source upsert after snapshot then identical replay; liquidation `PARTIAL_AGGREGATED` never promoted to complete; liquidation gap propagation/degradation/veto; missing source time never replaced by `as_of`; canonical serialization ordering/time/decimal/null stability and repeatable snapshot digest; all V1 vetoes and soft-degrade cases; all six directional pattern structures in MATCHED/PARTIAL_MATCH/CONFLICTED/NOT_MATCHED plus `NOT_CONFIGURED`; 5 bullish versus 2 bearish anti-voting; missing flow remains UNKNOWN and missing IV remains null; Fake and Recorded Jev deterministic paths; clear-input no-call, conflict call, invalid nested JSON/schema, invented Evidence ID, timeout, NOT_CONFIGURED, budget and forbidden instruction; durable JevReview before decision; immutable persistence/supersede/status chain/source refs/version/Git SHA; retry/dedup; process death during evaluation and after snapshot but before final commit; Real Jev unavailable plus required HIGH conflict fails closed; Phase 9 package import/schema scan has no RiskEngine, PositionSizing, Execution, Order or position-management dependency; no partial decision, cursor skip, future leakage or secret leak. A 10–15 minute local integration run uses a Stage 1 fixture stream, PostgreSQL, Fake/Recorded Jev, revalidation and failure/restart injection, recording memory, CPU, DB query volume/growth, queue depth, latency and duplicates. No real-provider Phase 9 network test or 60-minute public-source soak is a V1 gate.

Acceptance does not measure win rate, PnL, Sharpe or predicted profitability. Final state fields are `PHASE9_IMPLEMENTATION_COMPLETE`, `PHASE9_DETERMINISTIC_REPLAY_PASS`, `PHASE9_FAILURE_SEMANTICS_PASS`, `PHASE9_PERSISTENCE_AUDIT_PASS`, `PHASE9_RUNTIME_INTEGRATION_PASS`, `PHASE9_REAL_JEV_INTEGRATION_STATUS=NOT_CONFIGURED|PASS|BLOCKED_EXTERNAL`, and `PHASE9_READY_FOR_PHASE10`. Phase 10 readiness is false if replay fails, missing is coerced to neutral/zero, indicator voting is used, Jev receives trading authority, decision history mutates, duplicate decisions occur, provenance fails, restart corrupts state, a veto is bypassed or AI failure defaults to approval. It may be true with Real Jev NOT_CONFIGURED and auxiliary Options/on-chain PARTIAL when the Python path is deterministic, degradation explicit, Fake/Recorded Jev tests pass, audit is complete and runtime remains bounded. Phase 10 would begin paper-only under a separate authorization.

## Phase 9 / Phase 10 machine contract

The Phase 9 package dependency graph must not import or depend on `RiskEngine`, `PositionSizing`, `Execution`, `Order`, or position-management modules. `DecisionCandidate` schema rejects `entry_price`, `leverage`, `position_size`, `stop_loss`, `take_profit`, `order_type`, `account_balance`, `private_position`, and `execution_instruction`; these are forbidden fields, not nullable extensions. A future static import/schema test enforces this boundary.

A future Phase 10 consumer accepts a candidate only if all are true at consumption time: `eligible=true`; effective lifecycle status is `ACTIVE`; `valid_until > now_utc`; `decision_policy_version` is recognized and supported by that consumer; and `input_snapshot_hash` is present and valid. Any missing, expired, unsupported or contradictory field rejects consumption closed. This is an interface contract only; Phase 10 behavior is not part of this design implementation.
